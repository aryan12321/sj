The goodbye that

Chapter III · The details that stayed

It was never just
about seeing you.

Some details do not ask for attention. They simply stay.

Your voice.

There is something about it that can follow me long after the conversation ends. Some voices are heard. Yours has a way of lingering.

The way your hair falls.

That effortless movement, the way a few strands frame your face, and how even a small gesture can become a detail worth remembering.

Your rawness.

Unfiltered. Real. Not polished into somebody else's idea of how a woman should be. That rare feeling of meeting a person who seems to have kept hold of herself.

Maybe what stayed most was the feeling of you being unmistakably you. Not a perfect character written for somebody else's approval. A real person, with her own edges, laughter, moods, and magic. That kind of realness is difficult to forget.
Chapter IV · A question without an answer

What if life had
taken another turn?

In this world, there are structures, circumstances, and realities that cannot be wished away by beautiful sentences.

So I sometimes imagine a parallel world.

Not because this one is unreal, but because imagination is the one place where a road not taken can still be travelled for a while.

In that universe, perhaps we meet without the complicated footnotes. Perhaps there is no need to measure every sentence or hide every smile. Perhaps we simply get to know each other, slowly and honestly, and discover what the world might have looked like if the timing had been different.

I remember the first time I held your hand, how an ordinary gesture somehow became a moment my mind kept safe. Some memories do not need a grand setting. They simply stay warm.

I remember Saryu Mela too. I was sad when we met there, because I knew it might be difficult to meet you again easily. It is a peculiar kind of ache: being glad to see someone, while already missing the chance to see them again.

Since then, we have walked through some genuinely difficult stretches together. There were moments when your emotional world felt heavy, when questions married life got complicated, and when pregnancy brought hardest time and teary eyes. And way of talking were almost none. But, we kept holding hands with Teamchat and intercom. Koi aur hota to is sab me bikhar chuka hota. They were real hard times, and the journey has had its ups and downs.

But we kept finding ways through. We listened each other issues, we tried, we dealt with one thing at a time. Somewhere along the way, a bond that has been tested by life and, in its own way, made stronger.

I remember your voice, the way your hair frames your face, your spectacles, and the athletic ease in the way you carry yourself. Most of all, I remember your raw, unfiltered way of being yourself. You never seemed manufactured for other people's approval, and there is something quietly beautiful about that.

I do not know where this journey will lead. Life is not a story where we get to choose every turn, and there are realities that cannot be wished away. I am not writing this to predict an ending or ask you for an answer. I only want to be honest about the place this story has found in me.

And yes, I will probably keep imagining that parallel world.

Maybe it is made of small scenes instead: coffee forgotten mid-conversation, a shared joke that makes no sense to anyone else, your hand resting in mine, a wish to see your bare back and kiss that agains slowly, and a closeness that feels easy because it is wanted by both of us. Nothing staged. Nothing to prove. Just a moment that gets to be ours.

Perhaps the parallel world is not a plan at all. Perhaps it is simply a place where I can keep the impossible things gently, without asking reality to become something it is not.

Whatever comes next, I am grateful for the parts of this journey we have already shared: the laughter, the hard conversations, the unexpected tenderness, and the strength it took to move through the difficult days. I do not know the destination. I only know that the road has mattered.

I do not know whether that other universe exists anywhere beyond imagination. But I know I will keep returning to it sometimes, not to escape the life we have, but to give a little space to the life I once wondered about.
